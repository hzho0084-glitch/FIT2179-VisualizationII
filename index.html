Australian Sports

How participation, geography, funding and spectatorship shape Australian sporting culture.

Key story: sport in Australia is not one single pattern. Community pathways, state identity, major events and public investment all shape how people participate and engage.

1. How do Australians enter different sports?

School sport is a major entry point, but family influence, media exposure and friends also shape the pathway from casual interest into local clubs or spectatorship.

2. Which sport dominates each Australian state?

This choropleth map colours each state or territory by its associated dominant sport. Hovering over a region shows the state and leading sport.

3. How does sport preference change by age and gender?

This faceted heatmap compares participation index by age group and gender. It helps show whether a sport is concentrated in younger groups or remains broad across age groups.

4. How has attendance changed over time?

This streamgraph-style area chart shows how attendance shifted across sports between 2019 and 2025, including disruption around 2020 and the recovery afterwards.

5. How do funding and medals compare?

This connected scatter plot compares high performance funding with major event medal outcomes. Each sport is connected from 2019 to 2025.

6. Where are fans lost between awareness and participation?

The funnel chart shows the shrinking audience from basic awareness to active participation. This highlights the challenge of turning spectators into players.

This ranked dot plot compares venue utilisation across major Australian sports venues. The horizontal position shows average utilisation, while circle size represents annual attendance.

8. How mixed is each state’s sporting landscape?

This normalised stacked bar chart compares the share of multiple sports within each state. It shows whether a state is dominated by one sport or has a more balanced sporting mix.

9. Which sports gained the most funding?

This slope chart shows changes in high performance investment from 2019 to 2025. Steeper upward lines indicate faster funding growth.

10. Which sports have the largest participation footprint?

This bubble matrix summarises participation across states and sports. Larger circles represent larger participant counts, making it easier to see which sports have the strongest footprint.

Conclusion

Australian sport is shaped by region, infrastructure, school pathways and elite performance systems. AFL and Rugby League show strong geographic identities, while sports such as swimming, basketball and cricket connect participation with national and international performance stories.
